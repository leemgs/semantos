\begin{abstract}
Language models are increasingly proposed as kernel and database tuners, yet their value over an equally informed optimizer, and the safety of applying their proposals, are rarely measured with independent outcomes. We present SemantOS, a control loop that separates evidence (a typed interaction graph and retrieved traces), proposals (a retrieval-augmented reasoner emitting multi-knob bundles), and enforcement (a runtime that validates, vetoes or stages a complete bundle). We make three contributions. First, we specify a bundle-level control contract and implement it as a dry-run REST prototype together with a separate bounded per-thread actuator. Second, we give an expected-cost identity for gated acceptance and a class-conditional rank gate whose conditioning and exchangeability assumptions are explicit. Third, we define a role-separated evaluation protocol with raw-log verification and apply it to Linux timer-slack/affinity interventions (8,000 grid events, 80 staged executions). Equally informed non-LLM selectors both choose the default configuration, so held-out replay shows no improvement; a frozen gate accepts 5 of 25 and 4 of 24 unsafe runs under a period shift, exceeding its nominal level; and all 80 staged executions restore both original settings. These results show that the protocol exposes unfavorable outcomes rather than hiding them. Four open-weight 7--14B LLMs given the same frozen evidence do not improve on the default (one is worse on replay), and 16 of 28 quantitative explanation claims misstate that evidence despite valid citations.
\end{abstract}
\textbf{Keywords.} Kernel tuning; knowledge representation; selective prediction; provenance; experimental methodology.
