\begin{abstract}
SemantOS investigates a kernel-tuning control loop combining telemetry, a typed interaction graph, retrieval-augmented language-model recommendations, and independent enforcement. We distinguish a dry-run REST prototype from a separate, bounded per-thread actuator. We correct the expected-cost identity and state a class-conditional rank gate with explicit assumptions. Raw logs support local baseline measurements, timer-slack/affinity interventions, and a prospective role-separated follow-up with 8,000 grid events and 80 staged executions. Equally informed non-LLM selectors both choose the default control, producing no improvement in paired held-out replay. A frozen gate misses 5 of 25 and 4 of 24 unsafe evaluation runs, emphasizing that its exchangeability assumptions cannot be presumed. All 80 staged executions restore both original settings after completion or early stopping; this establishes narrow restoration behavior, not production safety. Exact-input model-ablation and context-deletion tools are provided, but model access was unavailable, so LLM benefit and explanation faithfulness remain unresolved. Externally supplied aggregates are separated from the reproducible evidence. No human-study outcome or drift-recovery guarantee is claimed.
\end{abstract}
\textbf{Keywords.} Kernel tuning; knowledge representation; selective prediction; provenance; experimental methodology.
