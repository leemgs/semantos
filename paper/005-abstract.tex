\begin{abstract}
Language models are increasingly used to recommend system configurations from retrieved evidence, and their recommendations arrive with citations and natural-language explanations. Whether such advice adds anything beyond the supplied evidence, and whether the explanations state that evidence correctly, is rarely tested. We propose an audit protocol that gives an equally informed non-LLM baseline the same frozen evidence, ablates evidence sources, deletes cited versus matched uncited evidence to probe reliance, verifies every quantitative claim in an explanation against the evidence, and scores decisions on held-out outcomes labeled independently of any enforcement mechanism. We instantiate it with SemantOS, a testbed that separates evidence, LLM proposals and enforcement, on real Linux timer-slack and CPU-affinity interventions (8,000 measured events). Across ten LLMs, from 7B open-weight to hosted frontier models, evidence is necessary: without it, nine models mostly choose a worse configuration. It is also sufficient: with it, nine reproduce the table-lookup baseline's choice and none improves on it. Explanations cite valid evidence yet misstate it: 16 of 28 audited quantitative claims from local models and 25 of 69 from hosted models are wrong, misattributed, overstated or in the wrong unit, and without evidence models invent meanings for opaque configuration identifiers. Deletion reveals citation-specific reliance for only one model. On the same testbed, a calibrated risk gate misses up to 20\% of unsafe runs under a period shift. We release prompts, raw responses, annotations and measurement logs.
\end{abstract}
