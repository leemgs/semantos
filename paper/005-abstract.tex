\begin{abstract}
SemantOS investigates a kernel-tuning control loop combining telemetry, a typed interaction graph, retrieval-augmented language-model recommendations, and independent enforcement. This revision distinguishes the implemented prototype from the intended deployment system. The artifact provides a dry-run bundle gate, provenance-carrying recommendations, and a class-conditional rank threshold with explicit assumptions. We replace unsupported performance and drift-recovery claims with a corrected expected-cost identity, an auditable measurement procedure, and a matched-information evaluation protocol. We also report newly collected baseline timings for three local microbenchmarks, with raw operation logs and repeated-run uncertainty; these measurements do not establish tuning gains. A further 9,600-event experiment measures actual per-process timer-slack and affinity interventions; a training-selected configuration matches the default control, with no selected-policy gain. Whether language-model reasoning improves on a conventional optimizer supplied with the same graph, traces, and budget remains an open empirical question. A separate author-reported hardware study describes a 14\% aggregate P95 reduction; raw logs and the aggregation rule were not supplied, so this estimate is not independently reproduced. No production-safety guarantee or human-study outcome is claimed.
\end{abstract}
\textbf{Keywords.} Kernel tuning; knowledge representation; selective prediction; provenance; experimental methodology.
