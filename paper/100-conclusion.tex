\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
A rationale that cites evidence promises a good decision, true claims about the evidence, and reliance on what it cites. Auditing them separately for LLM configuration advice, we found that they came apart. Ten LLMs needed the evidence to choose well but added nothing beyond a selector that reads it; their citations were valid but one-sided (252 of 260 cited runs back the chosen configuration); 41 of 97 audited claims misstated validly cited evidence, mostly alongside a correct decision; and deletion showed citation-specific reliance for only one model.

For NLP evaluation, these results suggest three practices. First, citation validity and passage-level support are not enough: an explanation can cite valid items, omit the contrary ones and still misreport what it cites, so attribution should be checked claim by claim and for what is left out. Second, the errors that data-to-text research documented for tables, namely wrong numbers, superlatives, ranges and overstated regularities, persist in the free-text rationales of instruction-tuned models of every size we tested, and deserve targeted checks. Third, LLM decision support should be compared with a baseline given the same evidence, reporting decision quality and rationale correctness separately. Next steps are tasks where documentation priors could help, and stronger automatic claim checks.
