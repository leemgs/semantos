\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We audited evidence-grounded LLM configuration advice with a protocol that holds the evidence fixed across LLM and non-LLM advisors, ablates and deletes it, verifies explanation claims against it, and scores decisions on independently labeled held-out outcomes. On real Linux interventions, ten LLMs needed the evidence to choose well but added nothing beyond a selector that reads it, and their explanations frequently misstated the evidence they validly cited. A calibrated gate on the same testbed missed more unsafe runs than its nominal level under a period shift. Citation validity, nominal calibration and a correct final choice are each weaker evidence than they appear; the protocol and released data make these gaps measurable.
