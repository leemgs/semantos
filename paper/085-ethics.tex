\section*{Ethics Statement}
\label{sec:ethics}
\paragraph{Intended use and risks of the findings.}
The paper studies how far users can trust LLM rationales that cite supplied evidence. Its main risk is misreading in either direction: treating valid citations or a correct final choice as evidence that a rationale is accurate, which our results argue against, or reading the audit as evidence that LLM configuration advisors are safe or effective, which it does not show (see Limitations). We recommend that LLM advice affecting real systems be paired with claim-level checks of its rationale and with enforcement that does not rely on the model.

\paragraph{Data, annotation and models.}
The data are timing measurements of local software workloads, synthetic configuration labels, and model responses to one fixed technical prompt; they contain no personal data and involve no human participants. The explanation claims were annotated by an author; no annotators were recruited or paid. All models were used for research inference under their licenses: Apache 2.0 (Qwen), the Llama 3.1 and 3.3 Community Licenses, MIT (Phi-4, DeepSeek-V3.2), OpenMDW-1.1 (Nemotron) and the Gemini API terms. We release code, measurements, prompts, responses and annotations under Apache 2.0; the stored responses remain subject to each model's terms.

\paragraph{System safety.}
The REST runtime performs no kernel writes; the experimental adapter changes only its owned child thread's timer slack and affinity, restores both settings, and performs no host-wide writes. Broader interventions require an isolated testbed, documented rollback limits and appropriate authorization.

\paragraph{Compute.}
The study used about 3.2 CPU-hours of local inference without a GPU, 150 hosted API calls costing about 0.5\,USD, and seconds of kernel measurement (Appendix~\ref{app:prompt}).

\paragraph{Use of AI assistants.}
AI assistants were used for writing and debugging code, for drafting and restructuring prose, and for locating and checking references; the authors reviewed all generated content and verified every reference against its publisher or arXiv record. The explanation claims were annotated by an author, not by an AI assistant. Every reported number is produced by released scripts from raw logs whose SHA-256 hashes are recorded, so that it can be recomputed independently; no generated value substitutes for an unavailable observation.
