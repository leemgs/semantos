\section{Motivation and Challenges}
\label{sec:motivation}
Kernel tuning is context dependent, its parameters may interact, and latency,
throughput, fairness, and safety objectives can conflict. These properties
motivate three testable hypotheses rather than assumed empirical observations.

\paragraph{H1 -- Context sensitivity.} A configuration selected for one
workload family or hardware class may regress another. The evaluation therefore
reports each workload--server cell and uses held-out workload families rather
than only pooled averages.

\paragraph{H2 -- Inter-parameter dependence.} Some changes may be beneficial
only as a bundle. SemantOS represents candidate relationships as typed,
evidence-backed graph edges, but does not call them causal. Each published edge
must identify the source runs and survive a held-out interaction test; the seed
graph is empty until such evidence is imported.

\paragraph{H3 -- Multi-objective trade-offs.} Improving latency can reduce
throughput or increase guard violations. We therefore retain all operating
points and reserve ``Pareto frontier'' for a nondominated envelope obtained by
sweeping each method under comparable budgets.

Together these hypotheses motivate context-aware telemetry, explicit graph
provenance, atomic multi-knob actions, and independent runtime enforcement. The
protocol in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} is designed to falsify, not encode,
their expected benefit.
