\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\paragraph{Task and generality.}
The main audit covers one grounded-advice task with four candidate labels, English prompts and a small numeric evidence set that determines the best choice, and the default is best on the test grid. It can therefore expose missing added value and misreported evidence, but it cannot show that an LLM adds value on harder tasks, and its error rates need not transfer to domains with textual evidence (manuals, logs, documentation), longer contexts or other languages. The configuration labels are opaque by design; with descriptive labels, models' priors could help or hurt differently. Three further evidence instances from the same host and workload family (Appendix~\ref{app:instances}) vary the values, labels and session. Only the hosted models were rerun on them, and their explanations were scored by automatic checks, not verified labels. A second domain, SQLite settings measured for this paper (Appendix~\ref{app:sqlite}), reproduces selective citation and misstatement. It has one workload and one machine and covers only the hosted models, and its explanations were read with an AI assistant rather than verified by annotators.

\paragraph{Annotation.}
Claim labels were drafted with an AI assistant and verified by one author; agreement with two independent blind annotators who are not authors is high (Cohen's $\kappa=0.92$ and $0.86$; Fleiss' $\kappa=0.85$ with the verified labels), but rests on few annotators and a small sample (97 claims). The annotators labeled claims that had already been extracted, so the extraction step (which statements count as claims) was not independently validated, and only quantitative or comparative claims were audited. Segmenting explanations into claims and deciding when a claim is ``overstated'' involve judgment; we fixed both rules in advance (a list counts as one claim, a comparison as another; ``consistently'' is overstated when a configuration is lowest in 8 of 10 blocks), and supply every label with its check so that the annotation can be repeated or automated.

\paragraph{Models and prompting.}
The main audit uses one system prompt and output schema at temperature~0. A rerun of the hosted models with a paraphrased prompt, reordered evidence, no citation cap and sampling (Appendix~\ref{app:robust}) leaves decisions and one-sided citation unchanged, but scores its explanations only with automatic proxies, not verified labels, as does the mitigation rerun (Appendix~\ref{app:mitigation}), whose checks see only two error types; local models and evidence formatting were not varied, and reasoning was toggled only within four hosted models (Appendix~\ref{app:reasoning}); the two Gemini models reasoned by default in the main audit. Local explanations were byte-identical across seeds, and five greedy seeds are not independent samples. Local models were 4-bit quantized; reruns of Llama-3.1-8B and Phi-4 with bf16 weights and of Qwen2.5-7B at 8 bits reproduce the decisions in all evidence and no-evidence variants and the explanation errors recur, but Phi-4 no longer changes its choice under cited deletion (Appendix~\ref{app:precision}); Qwen2.5-14B could not be rerun at full precision. Hosted models were served by several providers, could not be hashed, varied across seeds and are not reproducible bit for bit; DeepSeek-V3.2's providers ignored the explanation length limit. The six-citation cap and the 400-character limit shape what models cite and write; the robustness rerun shows that the citation prefix depends on the cap.

\paragraph{Reliance.}
Matched deletion measures the sensitivity of a decision to cited evidence, a form of self-consistency, not faithfulness to internal computation. In the main design it was non-estimable for half of the models, because they cited the only item of its kind; the redesign of Appendix~\ref{app:reliance} is estimable for most, but its controls still differ in which blocks remain, and splitting the table changed one model's choice.

\paragraph{Effect on readers.}
We show that rationales misstate checkable facts, not that readers are misled by them; no human-subject study was run. The supplement includes a between-subjects protocol prepared for pre-registration (misstated, minimally corrected or no rationale; evidence probes with computed answers), its materials and analysis code, for future work.

\paragraph{Measurement scope.}
All measurements use one periodic workload family on one shared host, with data roles separated by period rather than by workload family or hardware; run-level intervals do not account for uncontrolled interference or correlated system state and are unadjusted for multiple comparisons. Measured graph induction emits no edge, so the graph item is uninformative in this task. Selector, BO and RL comparisons replay recorded runs rather than executing closed loops, and because control is best they can reveal only losses. The telemetry prototype's scheduler and block-I/O proxies are not end-to-end request latencies, and the local benchmarks in the appendix do not substitute for application workloads such as web serving, databases or GPU inference.

\paragraph{System and safety scope.}
The REST runtime is a dry run that never writes kernel settings, and the separate actuator changes only an owned child thread's timer slack and affinity; neither establishes traffic isolation, a deployed multi-knob actuator or recovery from irreversible effects. The reasoner awaits model samples synchronously, and its logs are append-only files without tamper resistance. Prompting a model to respect graph edges does not enforce them; only the runtime's explicit constraints are enforced. The rank gate's assumptions fail under drift, selective labeling or a score updated with calibration data, as the period-shift result shows, and the gate replay neither verifies deployment assumptions nor establishes recovery. We therefore make no LLM-superiority, deployment-efficacy, no-worse-than-default or production-safety claim; Section~\ref{sec:protocol} and Appendix~\ref{app:testbed} describe the comparisons and engineering such claims would require.
