\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\paragraph{Task and generality.}
The audit covers one grounded-advice task with four candidate labels, English prompts and a small numeric evidence set that determines the best choice, and the default is best on the test grid. It can therefore expose missing added value and misreported evidence, but it cannot show that an LLM adds value on harder tasks, and its error rates need not transfer to domains with textual evidence (manuals, logs, documentation), longer contexts or other languages. The configuration labels are opaque by design; with descriptive labels, models' priors could help or hurt differently.

\paragraph{Annotation.}
Claim labels were drafted with an AI assistant and verified by one author; agreement with a second, blind annotator who is not an author is high (Cohen's $\kappa=0.92$), but rests on one additional annotator and a small sample (97 claims). Segmenting explanations into claims and deciding when a claim is ``overstated'' involve judgment; we fixed both rules in advance (a list counts as one claim, a comparison as another; ``consistently'' is overstated when a configuration is lowest in 8 of 10 blocks), and supply every label with its check so that the annotation can be repeated or automated.

\paragraph{Models and prompting.}
All models share one system prompt and output schema at temperature~0; we did not vary prompt wording, evidence formatting, decoding or reasoning modes, each of which could change both decisions and explanations. Local explanations were byte-identical across seeds, and five greedy seeds are not independent samples. Local models were 4-bit quantized and may differ from full-precision weights. Hosted models were served by several providers, could not be hashed, varied across seeds and are not reproducible bit for bit; DeepSeek-V3.2's providers ignored the explanation length limit. The six-citation cap and the 400-character limit shape what models cite and write, so the citation position effect in particular is confounded with the cap.

\paragraph{Reliance.}
Matched deletion measures the sensitivity of a decision to cited evidence, a form of self-consistency, not faithfulness to internal computation. It was non-estimable for half of the models, because they cited the only item of its kind.

\paragraph{Measurement scope.}
All measurements use one periodic workload family on one shared host, with data roles separated by period rather than by workload family or hardware; run-level intervals do not account for uncontrolled interference or correlated system state and are unadjusted for multiple comparisons. Measured graph induction emits no edge, so the graph item is uninformative in this task. Selector, BO and RL comparisons replay recorded runs rather than executing closed loops, and because control is best they can reveal only losses. The telemetry prototype's scheduler and block-I/O proxies are not end-to-end request latencies, and the local benchmarks in the appendix do not substitute for application workloads such as web serving, databases or GPU inference.

\paragraph{System and safety scope.}
The REST runtime is a dry run that never writes kernel settings, and the separate actuator changes only an owned child thread's timer slack and affinity; neither establishes traffic isolation, a deployed multi-knob actuator or recovery from irreversible effects. The reasoner awaits model samples synchronously, and its logs are append-only files without tamper resistance. Prompting a model to respect graph edges does not enforce them; only the runtime's explicit constraints are enforced. The rank gate's assumptions fail under drift, selective labeling or a score updated with calibration data, as the period-shift result shows, and the gate replay neither verifies deployment assumptions nor establishes recovery. We therefore make no LLM-superiority, deployment-efficacy, no-worse-than-default or production-safety claim; Section~\ref{sec:protocol} and Appendix~\ref{app:testbed} describe the comparisons and engineering such claims would require.
