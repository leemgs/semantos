\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Kernel tuning requires choosing configurations whose effects depend on workload, hardware, and the current operating state. A setting that improves one metric can worsen another. SemantOS explores whether an explicit representation of empirical interactions, combined with language-model proposals and an independent runtime, can make this process inspectable. The central research question is not whether a model can describe a configuration, but whether its decisions add value beyond the information already present in the knowledge base (KB).

The proposed architecture separates three responsibilities: evidence acquisition, candidate generation, and enforcement. A typed graph and retrieved traces expose the available evidence; a reasoner proposes a multi-knob bundle; a runtime checks the complete bundle and decides whether to veto it or enter a staged rollout. This separation makes the reasoner's contribution testable against graph-aware conventional optimizers.

\paragraph{Evidence status.}
The earlier artifact generated numerical outputs from constants matching manuscript tables, including Gaussian perturbations of specified effect sizes. Such a program checks a simulation's internal consistency, not an independently measured system. We have quarantined those artifacts and removed their quantitative claims from the current argument. The same applies to unsupported descriptions of a trained checkpoint, a 30-day deployment, and a 32-participant study. Their removal does not establish that no external records exist; it states that they are not evidence available in this artifact.

\paragraph{Contributions and scope.}
This revision provides (i) an explicit bundle-level control contract and a dry-run implementation, (ii) a corrected cost identity and a rank-gate result whose conditioning and assumptions are stated precisely, and (iii) raw-data measurement and evaluation procedures that separate independent outcomes from enforcement decisions. The new local measurements demonstrate the operation of the measurement pipeline only. Deployment efficacy and the incremental value of the LLM require the experiments in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.
