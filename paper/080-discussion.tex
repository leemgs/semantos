\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
The central idea is the separation of structured evidence, proposals, and enforcement. The artifact demonstrates a dry-run REST decision contract, a separate bounded per-thread actuator, raw measurements and non-LLM selector replay. Measured graph induction on the new training sweep emits no edge. It lacks a deployed VM-sysctl bundle actuator, traffic isolation, a verified trained checkpoint, and closed-loop LLM comparative results; our model audit is retrospective and small. Prompting a model to obey graph edges does not enforce those edges; independent validation is required. The current REST runtime enforces only its explicit example constraints.

The available telemetry prototype contains scheduler and block-I/O demonstration proxies, including timestamp-derived buckets; those are not end-to-end request latencies. They must not calibrate an empirical SLO claim. Actual workload drivers must record completion, failure and deadline data. The new local benchmarks do so for their narrow tasks but do not substitute for web serving, TPC-C, Kafka/Spark, GPU inference or audio pipelines.

The rank gate's assumptions can fail under drift, selective labeling, or a score model updated using calibration data. A canary can suffer harm before detection, and rollback cannot undo every side effect. No no-worse-than-default, catastrophic-regression prevention, Pareto superiority or universal safety claim follows from the prototype. These limitations define concrete measurements and engineering prerequisites for the next evaluation.
