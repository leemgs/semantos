\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The central design hypothesis is that typed dependency evidence can constrain
multi-knob recommendations while an LLM may help combine unfamiliar telemetry
and explain the result. The planned matched ablation is necessary to determine
whether the LLM adds recommendation quality beyond rules, BO, RL, or graph
lookup; explanation fluency alone is not evidence of optimization value.

The dependency graph records observed associations, not causal couplings.
Retrieved evidence may be stale, uncertainty may be miscalibrated under drift,
and staging can expose canary traffic before rollback. Accordingly, the safety
runtime is defense in depth rather than a correctness proof. Generality is also
unestablished until held-out workload-family experiments are completed from
traceable hardware logs. Production deployments, hard-real-time systems, and
cross-kernel portability remain future work.
